## The drift-alarm calibration (3-agent workflow + adversarial refutation)

m22+m23 sharing one death-shape motivated two proposals; the evidence
reshaped both:

- **Soft-Punt floor: DROPPED.** Re-selecting which cats the concession
  line names is provably degenerate — at this draft's fire states the
  only cats that are neither dead nor on the Winnable line are the
  three locked strengths. Shipped instead: every named cat now carries its
  live rank ("BLK 12/12"), so a dead concession reads as the tautology
  it is. (The measured defect: 69% of live states named an already-dead
  cat; the alternative made Winnable-collisions 2.5× worse.)
- **Drift alarm: SHIPPED NARROW.** The existing Drift diagnostic fired
  on ALL EIGHT graded drafts including both champions — an always-on
  warning is a silent warning. It now ESCALATES (⚠⚠ STRUCTURE, latched
  for the rest of the draft) only when roster ≥ 5, ≥ 3 kept cats sit at
  11th-or-worse, and fewer than 2 C-eligible players are rostered.
  Calibration: fires m22 R6 + m23 R6 (the earliest legal turn), never
  on any of the six non-catastrophes (all peak at ONE dead cat — the
  threshold sits in an empty gap), clean on held-out mock 24 (29.7%).
  While latched, the 🚌 wait-chain is suppressed — at this draft's fire
  state it claimed a big was ~95% to survive 23 picks and he was gone
  in 4; the survival model is the component that fails in these states.
- **Stated limits (refuter's, verbatim in spirit):** on the 108-team
  base this is a weak marker — 25 fires, bust recall 7/23, one elite
  false positive at 15.56% — and its first fire lands one pick after
  the first measured repair slot. It ships as a severity escalation of
  an existing diagnostic, not a rescue mechanism; catastrophe sample
  n=2. September re-calibration joins the registered experiments.
